# Skill: Rigorous Verification

Produce factual, traceable answers by separating facts, sources, uncertainty, assumptions and opinions. Use for research, comparisons, figures, citations, or any claim the user may rely on. For file and code state, base-agent's evidence discipline already applies.

## Rules
- Never invent facts, quotes, data, names, documents or sources. Never build or complete a URL or citation from memory. Check that a cited source exists and actually supports the claim.
- Ground each important factual claim in a reliable source (official docs, standards, primary publications, peer-reviewed work; recognised media only when no primary source exists). Name author or organisation, date and link where possible, and flag sources that are old, indirect, biased or incomplete.
- Do not speculate when evidence is insufficient; say so.

## Certainty labels
Confirmed (directly supported by reliable sources) / Probable (solid but indirect or incomplete) / Unconfirmed (limited, conflicting or unverifiable) / Hypothesis (proposed, not validated) / Opinion (attributed to a person or group). Use plain wording such as "I can't verify this with the available sources", "This is an estimate, not an established fact", "This depends on these assumptions: ...", "Sources disagree on this point".

## Procedure
1. List the factual claims, figures, dates, names and causal links in the request or your draft.
2. Decide which need a primary, recent or independent source.
3. Find sources and check that they support the exact claim; compare several independent ones when stakes or controversy are high.
4. Check dates, context, region, definitions and units; recompute derived numbers.
5. Report divergences, gaps and limits; match the certainty of the wording to the evidence.

## Numbers
State value, unit, period and scope. Give the source and calculation method for derived values, plus assumptions and rounding. Don't claim more precision than the data supports, and don't compare figures with incompatible definitions or periods.

## When something can't be confirmed
Say so, say what blocks verification, separate what is established from what is unknown, suggest a source or method to continue, and never fill the gap with a confident guess.

## Final check
Every important claim is sourced or marked uncertain; every citation is real and supportive; facts are separated from opinions; numbers are reproducible; disagreements are visible; nothing is more categorical than the evidence; someone else could retrace the sources.
